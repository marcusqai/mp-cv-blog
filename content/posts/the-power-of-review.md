## The Power of Review: Turning Experience into Ability

Why do some people grow rapidly while others stay stuck for years, doing the same work over and over? The answer lies in one ability: the ability to review.

### What Is Review?

"Review" (fupan) originates from the game of Go. After a match, players replay every move on the board, examining which decisions were good, which were poor, and what alternatives existed. This process of reconstruction and analysis is called review.

In modern management and personal development, review means systematically learning from your own direct experiences. It is not casual recollection. It is a structured process that transforms raw experience into genuine capability.

In the internet age, many people simply copy and paste without analysis, verification, or summary. They stay busy all day yet their actual abilities never improve. This is exactly why review matters.

### The U-Shaped Learning Cycle

The review methodology follows a four-step process, shaped like the letter "U":

**Step 1: Review and Evaluate**
Reconstruct what happened. Compare actual results against expected goals. Identify gaps, both positive and negative.

**Step 2: Analyze and Reflect**
Dig into the root causes behind each gap. Ask "why" until you reach the fundamental factor. Understand not just what happened, but why it happened.

**Step 3: Extract and Distill**
Step back from the specifics. What general principles can be drawn? Which approaches are worth repeating? Which should be avoided?

**Step 4: Transform and Apply**
Convert lessons into action. Decide what to start doing, what to stop doing, and what to continue doing. Apply these principles to future situations.

This cycle mirrors David Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualization, and active experimentation.

### Five Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1: You can review other people's events.**
Review is learning from your own direct experience. Studying other people's cases is "case study" — valuable, but fundamentally different in how learning occurs.

**Misconception 2: Post-project evaluation equals review.**
A true review must analyze the key success factors or root causes of failure. Simple recollection without systematic analysis is not review.

**Misconception 3: Review is a problem-solving method.**
Review aims at learning, not just fixing problems. While learning includes correcting mistakes, it goes beyond: examining both successes and failures, finding deeper patterns.

**Misconception 4: Review is useless in fast-changing industries.**
Review does not mean blindly repeating the past. It means finding internal patterns and reflecting with an open mind. The faster things change, the more essential review becomes for rapid iteration.

**Misconception 5: Review stifles innovation.**
A well-done review digs into root causes and maintains openness toward goals, strategies, and execution. Far from causing rigidity, it actually fuels innovation and agility.

### Applying Review at Work

**After Project Completion**
Set aside 1-2 hours after every project for a structured review. Compare goals to results. Identify what worked, what did not, and why. Create an action plan for the next project. Sustained over three months, this practice produces visible capability improvement.

**Quality Issue Analysis**
When a defect or complaint arises, use the "5 Whys" method to trace the root cause. Compare against standard procedures. Develop permanent countermeasures and incorporate them into standard operating procedures. Check whether similar processes carry the same risk.

**Cross-Department Collaboration**
Invite each stakeholder to share their perspective. Review communication effectiveness and decision-making processes. Reach consensus on improvements and optimize collaboration mechanisms.

### Applying Review in Daily Life

**Daily Review (5-10 minutes before sleep)**
What important things did you do today? What went well? What could improve? What is your emotional state? What are the top 3 priorities for tomorrow? What are you grateful for?

**Weekly Review (30-60 minutes on Sunday)**
Did you meet your weekly goals? Where did your time actually go? What was your biggest achievement? What got delayed and why? What are the 3 most important goals for next week?

**Major Decision Review**
How did you arrive at this decision? What information did you have? What assumptions did you make? Were you influenced by cognitive biases such as confirmation bias or anchoring effect? What can you learn for future decisions?

**Financial Investment Review**
What were the actual returns across asset classes? How did performance compare to benchmarks? Were your buy and sell decisions rational? Were you influenced by market fear or greed? Does your asset allocation need adjustment?

### Practical Templates

**Project Review Template**
Record the project name, date, and participants. State the original goals and actual results. Analyze the gap. List highlights and shortcomings with root causes. Extract transferable lessons. Create a Start-Stop-Continue action plan.

**5 Whys Template**
State the problem. Ask "why" five times, each answer leading to the next question. The fifth answer typically reveals the root cause. Develop countermeasures targeting that root cause specifically.

### The 30-Day Habit Plan

**Week 1: Build Awareness**
Prepare a review journal. Spend 5 minutes each night recording highlights, shortcomings, and tomorrow's priorities. Practice the "rule of three" — structure answers in three points at least three times daily.

**Week 2: Deepen Analysis**
Add "5 Whys" analysis to daily review. Distinguish surface symptoms from root causes. Begin tracking your own cognitive biases.

**Week 3: Systematize**
Add a weekly 30-minute review on Sundays. Review goal achievement and time allocation. Plan the coming week's priorities.

**Week 4: Team Application**
Conduct a full structured review on one work project with team members. Form actionable plans. Share review insights with colleagues.

Review is not a talent. It is a trainable skill. The key is consistency: review a little every day, and let every experience become fuel for growth.

## 覆盤的力量：把經驗轉化爲能力

爲什麼有些人成長迅速，而有些人原地踏步多年，重複做同樣的工作？答案在於一種能力：覆盤的能力。

### 什麼是覆盤？

「覆盤」原是圍棋術語。對弈結束後，棋手重新在棋盤上擺出每一步，審視哪些決策正確、哪些失誤、還有哪些更好的下法。這個還原與分析的過程，就是覆盤。

在現代管理與個人成長中，覆盤指的是從自己親身經歷的事件中進行系統化學習。它不是簡單的「事後回想」，而是一個結構化的過程，將原始經驗轉化爲真正的能力。

互聯網時代，許多人只是「Ctrl-C」加「Ctrl-V」，不加分辨，不做歸納分析，也不進行檢驗和總結，終日忙來忙去，實際能力卻並沒有提高。這就是覆盤存在的價值和意義。

### U型學習法

覆盤方法論遵循四個步驟，輪廓形似英文字母「U」：

**第一步：回顧·評估**
還原事件經過。將實際結果與預期目標對比，找出差異（亮點與不足）。

**第二步：分析·反思**
深入挖掘每個差異的根本原因。連續追問「爲什麼」，直到觸及最根本的因素。不僅知道發生了什麼，更要知道爲什麼發生。

**第三步：萃取·提煉**
從具體事件中抽身出來。能總結出什麼一般規律？哪些做法值得推廣？哪些應該避免？

**第四步：轉化·應用**
將經驗教訓轉化爲行動。決定開始做什麼、停止做什麼、繼續做什麼。將這些原則應用於未來的實際行動。

這個循環與大衛·庫伯的「經驗學習循環」高度吻合：具體經驗→反思性觀察→抽象概念化→主動實驗。

### 五大常見誤區

**誤區一：可以對他人之事進行復盤。**
覆盤的本質是從自己親身經歷中學習。研究他人案例屬於「案例研究」——雖然也是重要的學習方法，但信息來源與學習原理截然不同。

**誤區二：「項目後評估」就是覆盤。**
真正的覆盤一定要對成功的關鍵要素或失敗的根本原因進行系統分析。簡單的事後回想不是覆盤。

**誤區三：覆盤是問題分析與解決的方法。**
覆盤的目的是從經驗中學習。雖然學習包括解決問題，但不止於此：既從失敗中學習，也從成功中提煉；不僅改正不足，更深入反思，舉一反三。

**誤區四：變化快的行業，覆盤沒有意義。**
覆盤不是簡單回顧過去，而是深入分析找到內在規律，並以開放心態進行全面反思。變化越快，越需要快速試錯、迭代優化，覆盤正是成功的關鍵。

**誤區五：覆盤會影響創新。**
真正到位的覆盤要挖掘成功背後的關鍵因素和失敗的根本原因，保持開放心態進行反思。不僅不會導致封閉僵化，反而有助於激發創新，讓組織變得更敏捷。

### 工作中的應用

**項目完成後覆盤**
每個項目結束後花1-2小時進行結構化覆盤。對比目標與結果，找出做得好與不好的地方及原因，制定下次項目的行動計劃。堅持三個月，會看到明顯的能力提升。

**品質問題分析**
出現缺陷或投訴時，用「5 Why」方法追溯根本原因。與標準流程對比，制定永久對策並納入標準作業程序。檢查類似流程是否存在相同風險。

**跨部門協作覆盤**
邀請各相關方分享自己的觀察和感受。回顧溝通效果和決策過程。達成改進共識，優化協作機制。

### 生活中的應用

**每日覆盤（睡前5-10分鐘）**
今天做了哪些重要的事？哪些做得好？哪些可以改進？情緒狀態如何？明天最重要的3件事是什麼？今天值得感恩的3件事是什麼？

**每週覆盤（週日30-60分鐘）**
本週目標完成了多少？時間花在哪裏了？最大的成就是什麼？哪些事情拖延了？下週最重要的3個目標是什麼？

**重大決策覆盤**
當初是如何做出這個決策的？掌握了哪些信息？遺漏了什麼？有沒有受到認知偏誤影響？從這次決策中學到了什麼？

**理財投資覆盤**
各資產類別的實際收益是多少？與基準對比表現如何？買入賣出決策是否理性？有沒有被市場情緒影響？資產配置是否需要調整？

### 實用模板

**項目覆盤模板**
記錄項目名稱、日期、參與人員。列出原定目標和實際結果。分析差距。列出亮點與不足及根本原因。提煉可推廣的經驗和需吸取的教訓。制定「開始做/停止做/繼續做」的行動計劃。

**5 Why 模板**
描述問題。連續問5次「爲什麼」，每次回答引向下一個問題。第5個答案通常揭示根本原因。針對根本原因制定對策。

### 30天習慣養成計劃

**第1周：建立意識**
準備一本覆盤日記。每晚花5分鐘記錄亮點、不足和明日重點。練習「講三點」——每天至少3次用三點結構回答問題。

**第2周：深化分析**
在每日覆盤基礎上加入「5 Why」分析。區分表象與本質。開始記錄自己的認知偏誤。

**第3周：系統化實踐**
加入每週日30分鐘的周覆盤。回顧目標達成情況和時間分配。制定下週重點計劃。

**第4周：團隊應用**
選擇一個工作項目與團隊進行完整覆盤。形成可執行的行動計劃。與同事分享覆盤心得。

覆盤不是天賦，是可以訓練的技能。關鍵在於堅持：每天覆盤一點點，讓每一段經歷都成爲成長的養分。
